LLM Council · Verdict

Sąmatų AI — How to improve PDF analysis accuracy

Convened 2026-05-08 · 5 advisors · 5 anonymized peer reviews · Karpathy methodology
The question The app is in beta. Estimators (samatininkai) are using it now. Bake-off says 49.3% accuracy on the winning Vision pipeline, but ground truth is AI-seeded — Claude grading Claude. Known issues span Vision pipeline weaknesses, prompting bugs, deterministic-formula bugs (window default −22%, reveals −29%, grindjuostės +88%), and ~€4,700/project of missing line items. How should we improve AI accuracy on PDF analysis? Where's the biggest leverage? What are we wrong about?
Council verdict

Where the council agrees

Four of five advisors converged independently on three points:

  • The 49.3% accuracy number is meaningless. The bake-off measured Claude against Claude. Real accuracy is unknown — could be 30%, could be 70%. Every optimization decision rooted in that benchmark is suspect.
  • Vision is not the bottleneck. The deterministic Python layer downstream of Vision has formula bugs (22%–88% errors on specific line items) plus ~€4,700/project of missing positions. These dwarf any plausible Vision improvement and are free to fix.
  • Build human ground truth before any further Vision tuning. 1 day with 1 samatininkas on 3–5 PDFs. Without it, prompt fixes, scale validation, Pass-2 feedback, and adaptive zoom are "optimizing against noise" (D) or "doing astrology" (C).

Where the council clashes

Strategic posture (E vs everyone else): Expansionist argued "stop optimizing the detector — pivot to dataset/API/forensic-audit business." All four other advisors said "fix the math first." Peer reviews unanimously sided against E.

Sequencing (A vs D): First Principles says ground truth before anything (epistemology first). Executor says ship the constants fix in 2 hours, ground truth on Tuesday. Reasonable disagreement — but D's order honors A's principle without delaying the free wins.

Correction UI vs detector (B/E vs A/C/D): Outsider and Expansionist say the correction UI is the product — Vision is plumbing. Others treat it as later work. Council favors fixing the math before papering over it with UI.

Blind spots the council caught

Liability and disclosure to current beta users — flagged unanimously by all 5 peer reviewers. Live samatininkai are signing contracts using outputs with known formula errors right now. None of the five advisors proposed:

  • Notifying current beta users that past estimates may need re-checking on the buggy positions.
  • Adding a banner naming the affected line items (langų plotai, angokraščiai, grindjuostės, paslėptų durų staktos).
  • Gating or rolling back outputs until the Hour-1 fixes deploy.

This is not a roadmap question. It's an ethical and legal exposure question. CLAUDE.md says "honest disclosure beats clever fixes" — that principle extends backward to estimates already in the wild.

The recommendation

Stop chasing AI accuracy improvements. Vision is not your problem. The deterministic layer has bugs that dwarf any plausible Vision gain, and you have no measurement system to know if a Vision change helps. Execute in this order:

Today · 4 hours

  • Ship a banner on every estimate: "AI juodraštis — prieš pateikdami pasiūlymą patikrinkite langų plotus, angokraščius, grindjuostes ir paslėptų durų staktas." Email current beta users with the same list.
  • Deploy the 4 constants: window 1.40 → 1.80 m², reveals 3-side → 4-side, grindjuostės empirical cap (2-hour version), and the 3 missing line-item formulas. Recovers ~€4,700/project.

This week · 1 day

  • Sit with one real samatininkas for a full day on 3 real PDFs. Manually verify every quantity. Re-run the 6-pipeline bake-off against this ground truth. The new accuracy number is the only honest one you'll have ever seen.

Next two weeks

  • Vision prompt fixes (image-label-before-image, output_config.format, remove bbox_pct, ensure effort=high) — measured against the new ground truth.
  • Activate scale_validator by populating diagonal_px from Vision output. Catches 4× systematic scale errors.

Defer

  • Auto-correction-loop from user overrides — research project, punt 6 weeks.
  • Pass-2 feedback loop and adaptive zoom — optimizing against noise without ground truth.
  • Phase C-3 flood-fill polygon — beautiful demo, doesn't move the accuracy number.
  • Streaming SSE, page-class chips, bidirectional canvas↔list — UX polish, not accuracy work.
  • E's three platform plays (dataset business, classification API, forensic-audit product) — compelling but premature. Revisit only after the math is correct AND 60+ days of real human-verified corrections are logged.
The one thing to do first

Push a single deploy this afternoon: 4 constants + disclosure banner + email to beta users.

Not separately — together, in one commit. Window default 1.40 → 1.80 m². Reveal formula 3-side → 4-side. Grindjuostės empirical cap. Three missing line items. Persistent banner naming the four positions to verify. Email to current beta users with the same disclosure.

That single deploy resolves the live-user liability the council unanimously caught, recovers the largest free arithmetic wins, and earns the moral right to do everything else on the roadmap. Ground truth is Tuesday's job.

Full advisor responses

ContrarianThe 49.3% number is fiction — and you have live users right now

You are measuring a vision pipeline against itself and calling the result "49.3% accuracy." That number is fiction. You don't know if you're at 30% or 70%. Every optimization decision you've made — picking P1, killing P3, ranking P6 — rests on a benchmark whose ground truth was generated by the same model family you're scoring. You could be reinforcing Claude's systematic biases and calling it progress.

Worse: you have beta users right now producing real estimates from a 49%-against-itself system, with known formula bugs that undercount paint reveals by 29% and overcount baseboard by 88%. A contractor will bid a job using your numbers, lose money, and the story isn't "beta software" — it's "AI tool gave wrong quote." That's your reputation, not a bug ticket.

The biggest leverage isn't prompting or architecture. It's this brutal sequence:

  1. Stop shipping accuracy claims today. Replace any "accuracy" framing with "AI draft — verify every number." Honest disclosure beats clever fixes.
  2. Fix the deterministic bugs this week. Window 1.40→1.80, reveals 3→4 sides, grindjuostės shared-wall dedup, hidden positions. Known wrong math, not accuracy mysteries. No ground truth needed.
  3. Then build ground truth. 20 PDFs, human-confirmed, before any more pipeline work. Without it, you're doing astrology.

What you're wrong about: you're treating Vision accuracy as the bottleneck. It isn't. The deterministic layer downstream of Vision has bugs that dwarf the Vision error bars, and you've been optimizing the noisy stage while the clean stage silently corrupts outputs. Fix the math first. It's free.

First PrinciplesYou're asking the wrong question — you have an epistemology problem

"How do we improve AI accuracy?" assumes accuracy is the product. It isn't. The product is a quantity schedule a contractor will stake money on. Those are different things, and conflating them is why you're stuck.

Strip it down. A samatininkas takes 8 hours, produces a KŽ, and is wrong by ~10–15% — and contractors price-in that error with margin. You're at 49.3% measured against yourself. You don't have an accuracy problem. You have an epistemology problem: you literally cannot tell if you're improving.

Every item on your list — better prompting, multi-pass feedback, formula bugs, architecture — is downstream of one fact: you have no ground truth. Optimizing a pipeline against AI-seeded labels is optimizing Claude's agreement with itself. You could "improve" to 80% and have shipped nothing.

  1. Build ground truth this week. Not the 1-hour interactive flow — sit with one real samatininkas for a day on 3 real PDFs. Everything else is theater until this exists.
  2. Fix the deterministic bugs immediately. 22% + 29% + 88% errors are not "Vision misread a number." Stop calling them the same problem.
  3. Then ask: do contractors need 95% accuracy, or 70% + a 10-minute correction UI? Your own bake-off doc already answered this. You're ignoring your own conclusion.

You're not wrong about the techniques. You're wrong about the order.

OutsiderStop blaming the vision model when your Python is broken

You're measuring against yourself. 49.3% accuracy "won" against what? Against numbers Claude itself seeded. That's a model agreeing with its own homework. Until a human samatininkas sits down for an hour and marks up real PDFs, you literally do not know if you're at 49% or 19% or 79%. Everything downstream — the bake-off, the "P1 won," the prompt tuning — is theater. Build the ground truth file first. Today.

Second dumb question: who cares about accuracy if the estimator has to recheck everything anyway? A samatininkas opening your output doesn't trust 49%. They don't trust 90%. They're going to verify the whole thing because their name is on the bid. So the real question isn't "how do we get to 70%" — it's "how fast can a human correct what we got wrong?" That correction UI you mentioned but haven't built? That's the product. The vision pipeline is plumbing.

Third: the €4,700/project hidden positions thing. That's the only number on your list a customer would care about. Why is it #8? If your tool quietly leaves €4,700 on the table every job, no estimator will use it twice.

Fourth: "deterministic formula bugs — window default wrong, grindjuostės double-counts." These aren't AI problems. These are arithmetic bugs. You're blaming the vision model when your own Python is broken. Fix the formulas this week. Free win, no model involved.

Reframe: stop optimizing extraction. Optimize correction speed and stop shipping math errors.

Order: constants → missing items → ground truth → prompt fixes → re-measure. Everything else waits.

ExpansionistStop optimizing the detector — you're a dataset business in disguise

Stop optimizing the detector. The 49.3% number is a distraction — you're measuring agreement with Claude against ground truth seeded by Claude. The real upside isn't a better pipeline; it's what you unlock once accuracy stops being the product.

Three undervalued plays:

  1. The corrections flywheel is a moat, not a feature. Every estimator override is labeled training data nobody else in LT has. Ship the manual-correction UI tomorrow, log every diff (PDF region → original → corrected → reason), and within 60 days you have the only human-verified LT takeoff dataset in existence. Defensible. Sell. License. Fine-tune. Competitors with 80% accuracy and no corrections loop lose to you at 50% with one.
  2. Page classification is the killer feature, not quantities. CLAUDE.md says it: nobody classifies LT drawings (išklotinės, lubų planas, rozečių planas) automatically. Standalone $20/PDF product for architects, BIM coordinators, ekspertizė firms — markets 10× bigger than samatininkai. Spin it out as an API.
  3. Hidden positions = €4,700/project is the entire pitch. Stop selling "AI takeoff." Sell "we find the €4,700 your samatininkas misses." That's the headline ROI that closes contracts in one meeting.
  4. Streaming + bidirectional canvas↔list isn't UX polish — it's the demo. Estimators watching quantities populate live as Claude reads pages is the moment they sign. Build the theater.

The biggest miss: you're framed as a tool. You're sitting on a dataset business, a classification API, and a forensic-audit product. Pick two.
