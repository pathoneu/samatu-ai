Where the council agrees

  • The EUR climax must die, but its replacement cannot be hallucinated kiekiai. Pin the example numbers in the prompt itself.
  • The current ROWS constant fails the project's own CLAUDE.md §6 validation (lubos 148.6 m² on a 72 m² butas; walls 5.4× floor area).
  • The medium constrains the message. Babel-CDN + single-file = no room for gradient meshes, blob SVGs, or 600-line Tailwind soup.
  • Discipline beats creativity: allowlist + exact-string fix table + grep-based self-audit.

Where the council clashes

Expansionist vs. everyone else. The "Bring Your Own Kainynas" sandbox is rejected 4-of-5 by reviewers: inverts the funnel, demands ground truth the project doesn't have, 60% effort on an unvalidated interaction = wrong sprint.

First Principles vs. tactical advisors. Should we ship the prompt at all before 5 contractor calls? The chairman overrides the strict reading: a landing that honestly says "AI counts, you price" is shippable, but the promises (60 sekundžių, 14 359 €) that the product can't keep must go.

Outsider vs. Expansionist on jargon. "Kiekiai" itself is insider accounting language. The climax should be a work-outcome ("pasiūlymą iki vakaro, ne iki ketvirtadienio"), with kiekiai as proof beneath.

Blind spots peer review caught

  • Nobody verified the file's path / git state before writing line-anchored instructions. Anchors drift, fixes silently fail.
  • Cherry-picked kiekiai from a real PDF would be the same lie as the EUR climax — numbers must come from §6's known-good ranges, not from any specific real run.
  • No advisor questioned whether the hero animation should exist at all on a phone in a van.
  • The phone/email placeholders trap: tell a model to "fix" a tel:# and it invents a number. Must be explicitly forbidden.

The recommendation

Ship the prompt with three guardrails. Ignore the sandbox.

  1. Discipline over creativity. Allowlist + exact-string fix table + grep self-audit.
  2. Numbers come from the prompt, never from the model. Hard-coded 72 m² butas ledger that passes CLAUDE.md §6.
  3. The new headline is a work-outcome. "Pasiūlymą tu turi iki vakaro. Ne iki ketvirtadienio." Kiekiai are supporting evidence, not the promise itself.

The risk isn't shipping a landing — it's shipping a promise the product can't keep. Strip the promises, keep the page, get the contractor calls.

Full advisor responses

The Contrarian — 2× strongest pick, 0× blind spot

The single fatal flaw I'd bet money on: the prompt asks Claude Code to fix a 12-item checklist on a CDN-Babel single-file HTML, and the model will dutifully patch all 12 — while regenerating the pavyzdys numbers from training-data intuition instead of running them through the actual CLAUDE.md validation formulas. You'll trade an EUR-climax lie for a kiekiai-climax lie.

Concrete traps: (1) Hallucinated kiekiai are worse than the EUR bug — hard-code the numbers. (2) "Fix privacy microcopy" → model writes a new false claim; force exact wording. (3) Single-file + Babel CDN + "distinctive" → gradient meshes, blob SVGs, 600-line Tailwind soup; cap it. (4) No acceptance test — require literal grep/assertion at the end. (5) Dead tel:/WhatsApp — the model will invent a number; forbid placeholders explicitly.

The First Principles Thinker — 1× strongest pick

You're solving the wrong problem. The unit of work isn't the HTML — it's a single demonstrable claim a tired contractor believes in 8 seconds on a phone in a van. "Pivot from EUR to kiekiai" is a correction, not a claim. A samatininkas with 20 years of intuition does not wake up wanting a quantities extractor. He wakes up wanting the 3 hours he loses every Sunday counting rooms back, or not losing the job because he quoted slow.

The first-principles question: what specific pain, in the contractor's own language, does this app remove on Monday morning — and what is the smallest piece of evidence on the page that proves we actually remove it? If you don't know yet, the right move isn't a Claude Code prompt — it's five 20-minute calls with beta candidates this week. Fix the promise. Then prompt.

The Expansionist — 1× strongest, 4× biggest blind spot

The asymmetric upside: the landing IS the product's killer demo. Every LT competitor's landing is a feature list with a CTA. None dare let a visitor do the thing before signing up. Build a "Bring Your Own Kainynas" sandbox as the page's centerpiece: a pre-extracted kiekių žiniaraštis with chips filling one by one, an inline kainynas table the visitor types into (greyed defaults), a small "Kopijuoti į Excel" button. Trust loop made tangible: we do the boring counting, you own the pricing.

Tell Claude Code: invest 60% of build effort in this one interaction. Spreadsheet-cell feel, tab navigation, € auto-format. The sandbox is the moat. No LT competitor will copy it.

Peer review verdict: 4 of 5 reviewers flagged this as overreach. Sandbox inverts the funnel (asks Vytas to type prices before he trusts the tool), demands ground truth the project doesn't have (per 2026-05-07 prod test, ceiling-height detection still fails), and is a 2-week product feature rewritten as a landing fix. Rejected by the chairman.

The Outsider (Vytas, 52) — 1× strongest pick

I'm Vytas. 52. Truck smells like coffee and OSB. Phone in one hand. First thing I see: "14 359 €". Then somebody tells me the AI didn't make that — I did, with my kainynas. So what did the AI do? "Kiekiai." Quantities. The boring part. My žmona's cousin does that in Excel for €15/hr. "60 sekundžių." Bullshit. Close tab.

Nobody in my trade wakes up thinking "I wish someone would extract my quantities faster." We wake up thinking "I lost another tender because my pasiūlymas was three days late and Marius from Kaunas got it in by Tuesday."

The climax shouldn't be a number. The climax should be: "Tu turi pasiūlymą iki vakaro, ne iki ketvirtadienio."
