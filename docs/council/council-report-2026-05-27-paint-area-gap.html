Closing the paint-area gap on Berzas project

The question: How do I make Vision-extracted per-room perimeters from rasterized LT construction PDFs deterministic enough to close the remaining -0.78% gap on combined paint area? Current: 580.24 m². Contractor truth: 584.81 m². Or is this already noise-floor and should I ship?

Chairman's Verdict

Stop chasing -0.78%. Verify the formula match first — the gap may not exist.

▸ Where the council agrees

  • -0.78% on N=1 is statistical noise, not signal. Vision noise floor (±2-3%) > the gap. You can't detect a 0.78% improvement with a ±2.5% instrument.
  • Ground truth itself is uncertain. Different contractor → different number. Same contractor on a different day → different number. Paint sold in discrete buckets.
  • Bottleneck is sample size, not algorithm. N=1 cannot distinguish systematic bias from a lucky outcome.
  • CLAUDE.md §7 already prescribes confidence indicators (🟢🟡🔴) — quantified uncertainty is the closest thing to a real product extension.

▸ Where the council clashes

Productize variance vs. ship one number.

Expansionist: variance = product. Ship Konservatyvus/Tikėtinas/Optimistinis columns, confidence intervals, click-to-verify UI. Build a moat from uncertainty.

Executor + Outsider + 4 of 5 reviewers: LT contractors want ONE number to bid with. Range columns look like a refusal to commit and = liability against fixed-price competitors. Honest disclosure ≠ confidence intervals — it's a single number + ±3% badge.

▸ Blind spots the peer review caught

Critical — flagged by 4 of 5 reviewers
No advisor pursued the door-deduction formula match check. If contractor's 584.81 was computed with door_area = 1.89 m² (full opening), and we use 1.125 m² (opening − reveal at 0.15m), the 4.57 m² "gap" is purely a definitional mismatch:

14 × (1.89 − 1.125) = 10.71 m² of formula difference

That's MORE than the 4.57 m² residual. The "gap" might not exist at all — or might be larger once normalized.
  • Roof V1.5 has --use-gt-inputs analog (commit 8e97f23) — already separates merger correctness from Vision drift in your own codebase. Adopt the same eval split for interior paint BEFORE any optimization.
  • Stability test on Berzas alone — re-run the existing pipeline 5× on Berzas (~$2). Reveals whether 580.24 is a stable point estimate or itself wobbles ±2%. Determines if the gap is even measurable.

▸ The recommendation

Stop optimizing toward Berzas. The path forward:

  1. Verify formula definition with contractor — non-negotiable, before anything else.
  2. Re-run Berzas 5× as a stability test (~$2). Measures actual variance of point estimate.
  3. Adopt the roof pipeline's --use-gt-inputs eval split for interior paint.
  4. Get N=4 contractor-validated LT PDFs.
  5. Ship 580.24 with "tikrumas: ±3%" badge (CLAUDE.md §7 compliant).
  6. Approach #8 (Vision enumerates mm-dimensions → deterministic perim) ONLY IF systematic bias remains after N=4.

Do NOT productize variance ranges. LT contractors bid with single numbers.

Full advisor responses

The Contrarian — "p-hacking with LLMs"

-0.78% on ONE PDF is not a signal. It's noise that happens to look like a target.

Where's the rest of the test set? You have one contractor-validated project (Berzas). Your "584.81 m² truth" itself has uncertainty — did the contractor measure to the millimeter, or eyeball it? What's his run-to-run variance if you asked him to remeasure?

The fatal flaw: you're optimizing against a single point estimate as if it were ground truth, when your measurement instrument (Vision) has ±2-3% variance. You cannot detect a 0.78% improvement with a ±2.5% instrument. This is p-hacking with LLMs.

Worse questions avoided:

  1. What's variance on the next PDF? Approach #8 might score 580 on Berzas and 540 elsewhere.
  2. Is the contractor's 584.81 even right? Did he use the same door deduction formula (1.125 m²)? If 1.89, your "gap" is a definition mismatch.
  3. What does the customer pay for being wrong by 5 m²? Probably nothing — they re-quote on site anyway.

Push: freeze the pipeline. Run on 5-10 more contractor-validated PDFs. Report mean error AND σ. Customer-acceptable is almost certainly ±5%, not ±1%.

The First Principles Thinker — "what does 584.81 actually mean?"

The question isn't "how do I close -0.78%." The question is: what does 584.81 m² actually mean?

Your "contractor-validated truth" is one human's measurement. Ask the same contractor next week → 581 or 588. Different contractor → different again. Paint coverage itself varies ±15% by surface roughness, primer absorption, applicator technique. Paint is sold in 2.5L / 5L / 10L increments. Nobody buys 0.78% more paint.

You're tuning a deterministic pipeline to match a stochastic human measurement of an ambiguous physical reality, then declaring the residual a "gap." That's not a gap. That's noise you've labeled as signal because you have one data point.

  1. What decision changes at 580 vs 584? If nothing — same paint order, same quote, same crew — then -0.78% is a vanity metric.
  2. Where's your N? Cannot distinguish "0.78% bias" from "this PDF happened to land here" with N=1.
  3. What's the customer's tolerance? Probably 5%, not 0.78%.

Stop. Ship. Get N=10. Then re-ask this question with actual data.

The Expansionist — "the variance is the product"

You've hit the noise floor of your ground truth, not your system. Your 580.24 is statistically indistinguishable from his 584.81. A second contractor would give 587.2. A third, 578.9.

What you're missing: the variance is the product.

Run Vision 5x at higher DPI. Don't take the median to hide it — expose it. Ship "Svetainė: 30.5–32.5m (±0.5m)" as a first-class UI element. Now you have:

  1. Confidence interval per room. Competitors ship single numbers and lie. You ship error bars and tell the truth. That's a moat, not a bug.
  2. Targeted correction surface. Wobbly room → "tap to verify" badge → user draws polygon → variance collapses to 0.
  3. Pricing leverage. Konservatyvus / Tikėtinas / Optimistinis columns. Contractors quote high, win low.
  4. Data flywheel. Every user correction trains priors per room-type. In 6 months Berzas becomes irrelevant.

Quantified uncertainty + one-click correction is a category. Stop closing the gap. Productize it.

The Outsider — "you're measuring the wobble of your own ruler"

-0.78% isn't a gap. It's noise. You're measuring the wobble of your own ruler.

  1. One data point isn't a target, it's an anecdote. Inter-contractor variance on same PDF is likely 3-5%. You're chasing a precision your reference can't support.
  2. Customer experiences trust, not percentages. Spend next sprint on showing the work (per-room confidence, which rooms are shaky), not shrinking the residual.
  3. N-run median is the only rational approach — but only because it's diagnostic: which rooms are stable vs wobble. Flag wobbly ones as 🟡 for review.

Ship it. Move on. The next 0.78% costs more than the next feature is worth.
