## Claims (with sources)

- Feeding the raw SVG (vector XML) alongside the PNG raster (PNG+SVG) raised GPT-4o room-count accuracy on moderate-complexity CubiCasa5K plans from 0.75 (PNG-only) to 0.96, and raised room-label accuracy for all three models (Llama 3.2 11B: 0.71 to 0.96 on low-complexity plans) - i.e. a vector/text lane reconciled with vision beats raster-only for counting and labelling rooms.
  src: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03478
- Vector-only input is not a safe replacement for the raster: SVG-only dropped Claude 3.7 Sonnet room-count accuracy from 0.95 to 0.75 on low-complexity plans and drove Llama 3.2 11B to 0.00, because the smaller model hallucinated non-existent rooms by treating XML tags as subspaces - the raster must stay in the prompt and the vector lane should be reconciled against it, not substituted for it.
  src: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03478
- On 120 real floor plans, the best frontier multimodal models plateau at only 0.40–0.55 mean exact-match accuracy when counting doors, windows, bedrooms and toilets from a single rasterised image with one structured-JSON prompt; Gemini 3 Pro is best (0.51 mean accuracy, 16.0% mean MAPE), GPT-5.2 second (0.49, 19.4%), with door/window exact-match far lower than the mean (Gemini: 0.39 door, 0.34 window). This is the naive-VLM ceiling for symbol counting without tiling, zoom, voting or CV assistance.
  src: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04819
- Claude Opus 4.5 (the model family Pathon runs in production) scores 0.42 mean exact-match on floor-plan counting but only 0.16 on doors and 0.16 on windows, with 30.7% door MAPE and 37.3% window MAPE — while achieving the best bedroom MAPE of any model (2.8%) and 0.91 bedroom / 0.76 toilet exact-match. Window/door counts from Claude vision on unseen plans should therefore be treated as unreliable by default, whereas text-tagged room counts are near-reliable.
  src: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04819
- Counting targets anchored by text labels (bedrooms, toilets) are counted far more accurately than targets conveyed only by graphical symbols (doors, windows), because models fall back on OCR/tag reasoning; symbol conventions that vary across CAD standards and drafting styles are the specific cause of cross-style failure on rasterised drawings. This supports a text-lane-first strategy (room schedules/'eksplikacija', labels) with vision reserved for what text cannot supply.
  src: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04819
- Self-consistency voting on a VLM — sampling the same image ~20 times and taking the per-cell median of numeric values after aligning rows/columns by label clustering — raises numeric table-extraction accuracy over single-pass by 2.7%–23.1% relative (RMS-F1), with the largest gains on weaker models and on the denser, more stylistically diverse WB-ChartExtract set (Seed 1.6 Flash 35.08→43.17; Qwen3-VL 52.91→56.23; Llama 4 Scout 30.41→33.14) versus small gains on the easy ChartQA set (TinyChart 95.20→95.28). Caveat for Pathon: Table 1 also shows a single-pass frontier model (Gemini 2.5 Pro, 87.83 on WB-ChartExtract) beating every ensembled open/small model (best 56.23) — model choice dominated voting. Domain is charts, not drawings; transfer to dimension strings/room tables is plausible but unmeasured here.
  src: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27298
- Voting has a hard ceiling: it can only choose among candidates the model already produces, so systematic misreads are not corrected. After ensembling, residual errors are dominated by precise numeric value reading (Value Errors 19–40%, Missing 22–42% of errors) rather than structural/layout errors, and are worst where values must be inferred from continuous geometry (area/line charts: Llama 4 Scout drops from 48.39 on grouped bars to 13.94 on area). This is the analogue of dimension-string and small-text hallucination on drawings — voting will not fix a consistently misread dimension.
  src: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27298
- Current VLMs show a large 'zooming gap' on fine-grained perception: the SAME question answered from a micro-cropped region scores 16-25 percentage points higher than from the full image (ZoomBench Table 7: Qwen3-VL-8B 63.08% regional vs 37.87% global = 25.2pp gap; GLM-4.5V 73.61% vs 49.23%; Kimi-K2.5 73.25% vs 56.33%). On the OCR/'text and symbol recognition' dimension specifically, Qwen3-VL-8B goes from 47.97% (full image) to 69.11% (crop). The authors attribute this to attention dilution, not recognition failure — i.e. a crop/zoom pass over small dimension strings and room labels on dense A1 sheets is expected to recover reads the full-page pass misses. Caveat: measured on natural high-res photos and GUI screenshots, not architectural drawings.
  src: https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11858v1
- An external learned crop/zoom step delivers its largest gains when the base VLM sees the image at low resolution, and the gain shrinks sharply once the VLM already ingests a high-resolution image: on SmolVLM, DocVQA rises +18.51 points at 512x512 input but only +2.01 at 2048x2048 (InfographicsVQA +4.24 vs +3.88; TextVQA +8.23 vs +1.86). Implication for a drawing pipeline: feeding high-DPI rasters/tiles captures most of the benefit, and a zoom pass adds a smaller increment on top.
  src: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19820
- A cropping policy trained once (without fine-tuning the answering VLM) transfers to unmodified open and proprietary VLMs with double-digit gains on document/infographic reading: Qwen 2.5 VL DocVQA 69.52 -> 84.41 (+14.89) and InfoVQA 44.03 -> 55.95 (+11.92); GPT-4.1 nano DocVQA 48.16 -> 58.17 (+10.01), InfoVQA +11.42, V* +9.43. This is evidence that a small crop-proposal front-end can lift a frontier API model on small-text-dense inputs without touching the model.
  src: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19820
- Zero-shot frontier VLMs perform poorly on strict exact-match extraction of structured symbol/annotation entries (GD&T feature control frames: characteristic, tolerance, datum) from 400 expert-annotated 2D engineering drawings: GPT-4o scored precision 59.03% / recall 25.39% / F1 35.51% / hallucination 40.97%, and Claude-3.5-Sonnet precision 44.01% / recall 37.27% / F1 40.36% / hallucination 55.99% (hallucination defined as FP/(TP+FP)). I.e., 41–56% of the entries these models emitted were not in the ground truth — a measured hallucination ceiling for zero-shot VLM reading of dense annotated drawings (mechanical, not architectural).
  src: https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.03707
- A 0.23B-parameter open VLM (Florence-2-base) fully fine-tuned on only 400 expert-annotated drawings (8:2 split, so ~320 training drawings) beat the best zero-shot closed model on every metric, reaching precision 76.71% / recall 51.34% / F1 61.51% / hallucination 23.29% (Table 1, Exp-3) — relative gains of +29.95% precision, +37.75% recall, +52.40% F1 and −43.15% hallucination. Note the absolute ceiling: even fine-tuned, recall was only 51% and F1 61.5%, so ~half of ground-truth entries were still missed.
  src: https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.03707
- Vector decomposition hurts topological/spatial reasoning rather than helping it: on pathfinding, GPT-4o's valid-path score fell from 74.1 (PNG) to 63.9 (SVG-only) and 69.4 (PNG+SVG) on low-complexity plans and Llama collapsed from 25.9 to 5.4; the dominant error was assuming traversability across walls/non-adjacent rooms (100% of GPT-4o's invalid paths under SVG-only) - so tasks that depend on adjacency (which wall borders which room, door-to-room assignment) should not be expected to improve from dumping vector paths into the prompt.
  src: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03478
- The gain from adding vector input is model-dependent and, for the Claude family, not present on room counting: Claude 3.7 Sonnet went 0.95 to 0.90 (low) and 0.61 to 0.60 (moderate) under PNG+SVG on subspace counting (Table 1), while it did gain on moderate-complexity pathfinding (54.7 to 72.6 valid-match score) - a vector lane must be eval-gated per model/task rather than assumed to help.
  src: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03478
- Across 192 drawing-grounded QA pairs on 21 drawings there is a stable capability gradient: text extraction/OCR 0.70–0.95 accuracy, spatial reasoning 0.6 to high-0.7, instance counting worst; and the named counting failure modes (misread door swings, windows confused with openings/façade elements, hallucinated fixtures, missed instances in dense regions) persist on the very drawings where the same model's OCR is good — so good text reads are not evidence that geometry/symbol reads are correct.
  src: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04819
- The authors recommend that symbol-count features ship as assistive tools that surface candidates plus uncertainty (not autonomous extractors), that dedicated symbol detectors be coupled with general MMLMs, and that human corrections from real workflows feed active learning — but these are untested recommendations: the benchmark itself used a single fixed prompt, single rasterised PNG per item, no vector/CAD input and no multi-sheet reasoning, so it provides no measured gains for tiling, zoom, voting, ensembles or vector parsing.
  src: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04819
- Dispersion across repeated samples (relative median absolute deviation per cell) is a statistically significant but only weak-to-moderate predictor of extraction error — Spearman ρ between −0.30 and −0.37 against RMS-F1 (p<0.001) on WB-ChartExtract. It is usable as a confidence signal to rank which values a human should check first (confidence-ranked review), but it is far from a reliable per-value error detector and will leave many confident-wrong values unflagged.
  src: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27298
- Convergence detection (stop sampling once the aggregated table stabilises) preserves ~99% of the ensemble gain while cutting samples 1.24× (20.0→16.11 on WB-ChartExtract; +2.73 vs +2.76 RMS-F1), but on complex inputs only 27.7% of images had converged by K=20 (median stop K=6 on the easy ChartQA). Voting raises inference cost roughly 4–16× over single-pass; the paper's absolute cost stayed under $15 per benchmark, though with Opus-class pricing per page the multiplier matters more for Pathon than the absolute figure.
  src: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27298
- Single-pass VLM numeric extraction is highly non-deterministic on dense inputs: across 20 runs of a chart-specialised VLM (TinyChart) on WB-ChartExtract, 99.4% of charts produced at least one differing extraction and 49.0% produced tables of different dimensions run-to-run. Implication: a one-shot read of a dense drawing/table is a random draw, and run-to-run disagreement (including disagreement on how many rows/rooms exist) is itself a measurable signal to exploit for review triage.
  src: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27298
- Agentic inference-time zoom/crop tool loops yield only modest measured gains and cost ~10x inference time: giving Qwen3-VL-8B a zoom tool raised its 4-benchmark average (V*, HR-Bench-4K/8K, MME-RealWorld-en) from ~76.5% to 79.1% (+2.6pp), and dedicated agentic 'thinking-with-images' models (DeepEyes 75.5%, Thyme 74.0%, Mini-o3 76.1%) did not beat it; a single-pass model trained on 74K region-distilled samples reached 81.9%, i.e. training-time distillation matched or beat inference-time zooming at ~10x lower latency. Implication for a production pipeline: multi-round zoom loops are not a free accuracy lever; the gain is bounded and the cost is large.
  src: https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11858v1
- Fine-grained counting of small, densely packed objects is the dimension that zooming helps LEAST — it stays the hardest ZoomBench dimension even with crops, and the trained model only improved CountQA from 28.99% to 32.40% (+3.4pp). This is direct evidence that a zoom pass does not fix VLM symbol/fixture counting (outlets, switches, sanitary symbols) and that counting needs a different mechanism (detector or human count tool).
  src: https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11858v1
- Small, targeted, teacher-verified datasets beat much larger generic synthetic ones: 74K region-distilled QA pairs (with majority-voting consensus filtering by teacher models) trained Qwen3-VL-8B to a 68.12% overall average versus 63.24% for 500K Oasis synthetic samples, 65.84% for DeepEyes (47K), 66.13% for Thyme-RL (55K); even a 10K subset was competitive. Relevant to the data-strategy question: a few thousand high-precision, consensus-filtered crops-with-labels can outweigh large but noisy corpora.
  src: https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11858v1
- A learned (RL/GRPO) cropper that needs no human bounding-box labels beats training-free attention-based cropping (ViCrop) and UV-CoT on average, and the margin is largest with the stronger answering VLM: average 67.14 vs 59.67 (ViCrop) vs 60.64 (UV-CoT) on Qwen 2.5 VL, and 42.71 vs 41.35 vs 40.01 on LLaVA 1.5. The reward is derived from the VLM's own answer accuracy or answer log-likelihood, so no region annotation corpus is required.
  src: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19820
- The zoom front-end is cheap enough for a small team: a 256M-parameter cropping network trained on 124k image-question pairs (62k SFT + 62k GRPO, drawn from TextVQA/ST-VQA/DocVQA/InfographicsVQA) in roughly 27 GPU-hours on one A100, with inference at 2048px costing ~691 ms and ~1.7 GB versus 15-21 GB for ViCrop/UV-CoT.
  src: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19820
- Known failure ceiling and applicability caveat: a zoomed crop can actively mislead the answering VLM (the paper documents qualitative cases where the crop caused a wrong answer), and all evaluation is on English scene-text, document and infographic benchmarks (TextVQA, DocVQA, InfoVQA, ST-VQA, V*, HR-Bench) with no architectural drawings, so the gains on A1/A3 Lithuanian drawing sets are unmeasured and any crop/zoom pass should be evaluated for regressions, not assumed monotonic.
  src: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19820
