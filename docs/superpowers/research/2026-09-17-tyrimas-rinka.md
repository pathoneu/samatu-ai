# AI-assisted takeoff market: how vendors present AI results for human verification (excl. Togal/Kreo)

## Executive summary

1. Every incumbent's "AI" is one of two things: **select-one-symbol matching** (Autodesk, Bluebeam VisualSearch, STACK Autocount, Countfire, Trimble, Cubit) or a **one-sheet "first pass"** of walls/areas/counts (ConstructConnect/PlanSwift Auto Takeoff, STACK AI Takeoff, Buildxact Blu, Measure Square).
2. Verification UX has converged: markers on the sheet + a side list you click to zoom + checkbox/deselect before committing + manually adding misses with the normal count tool.
3. **No incumbent shows per-item confidence.** Sensitivity sliders (Bluebeam) or thresholds are the substitute; per-item confidence appears only in 2024–26 newcomers (Bobyard, Quotr, Drawer.ai, Aginera).
4. Vendors themselves instruct users to verify: On-Screen Takeoff says "It is critical that you set and verify Scale"; ConstructConnect says "trust but verify"; Trimble says estimators "identify false positives" with their own QA/QC.
5. The most repeated review complaint: symbol matching fails on busy, scanned, rotated or hand-annotated drawings, forcing a manual recount.
6. Accuracy claims are vendor-internal (Beam 99%, Buildxact 97%, Quotr 95–99%). Only Handoff H1 publishes a benchmark (81.6% vs 77–78% for humans, on 15 residential sets, its own benchmark).
7. Price bands: $39/mo (Easy Takeoffs) → $249–299/user/mo (STACK) → $1,250–3,000/seat/yr (Autodesk, PlanSwift, OST) → $8–25K/yr/trade (Beam done-for-you).
8. **Interior finishing is the blind spot**: only Bobyard (June 2026) and Kamai claim to read finish/door schedules; nobody documents tile height, skirting minus doors, reveals, per-room ceiling heights, or user-overridable assumptions.
9. Elevations, sections and wall developments are explicitly unsupported by the Auto Takeoff tools (OST, Buildxact).
10. EU: only DACH tools surfaced (monco.ai, Phase0, GRAVA connect — GAEB/VOB-oriented, no interior-finish takeoff evidence). Bidmatic (an adtech firm) and Kalkulus: not found.

## 2. Ten verification UX patterns

1. **Select one symbol → markers on every match.** Autodesk "detected symbol marker on the sheet" ([help](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/ENU/Takeoff-Takeoff/files/Symbol_Detect.html)); STACK "places counts on matching symbols" ([help](https://help-preconstruction.stackct.com/docs/fr/autocount)).
2. **Side list synced to the drawing (click-to-zoom).** Autodesk "click each detected instance… to zoom"; Bluebeam "Select any result to jump to its location" ([tutorial](https://support.bluebeam.com/user-manual/tutorials/workflow-visualsearch-count.html)); Cubit Scan Text "jump directly to where each instance… appears" ([release](https://buildsoft.com.au/blog/cubit-estimating-2026-july-release)).
3. **Checkbox accept/deselect before committing.** Bluebeam "Apply Count Measurement to Checked"; Autodesk "Deselect any items"; Bobyard "before committing results to their takeoff" ([2.0](https://www.morningstar.com/news/business-wire/20260408131586/bobyard-launches-bobyard-20-delivering-faster-takeoffs-a-consolidated-ai-workbench-and-a-platform-built-to-scale-across-trades)).
4. **Add misses with the ordinary count tool.** Autodesk Count tool; Bluebeam "Resume Count"; Countfire "adjusted in a couple of clicks" ([product](https://www.countfire.com/product/takeoff-software)).
5. **Subtractive checking — hide what's counted / hide background.** Countfire shortcuts T and B; "symbols removed drawings… make it obvious if a symbol has been missed" ([4 stages](https://help.countfire.com/en/articles/364279-the-4-stages-of-checking)); Countfire "hide busy PDF backgrounds". Only Countfire documents this; the other Countfire-style keyboard accept/next flow was **not found** elsewhere.
6. **Marked-up record drawings + audit trail as the deliverable.** Countfire "full audit trail… exactly what was counted and where"; Handoff H1 "marked-up drawing set showing what was detected where" ([Bricks & Bytes](https://bricks-bytes.com/ai/ai-construction-takeoffs-two-weeks-to-two-hours-handoff/)); Boon "tied back to the drawing for traceability".
7. **Sensitivity/threshold instead of per-item confidence.** Bluebeam "Sensitivity" slider; Aginera "confidence thresholds" ([comparison](https://aginera.ai/blog/ai-takeoff-software-comparison-2026)).
8. **Per-item confidence score to triage review** (newcomers only). Bobyard "Every detection carries a confidence score" ([finishes](https://www.bobyard.com/blogs/bobyard-finishes-trades-ai-takeoffs-launch/)); Quotr "Smart Matching scores every count" ([Quotr](https://quotr.ai/blog/ai-construction-estimating-software-buyers-guide/)); Drawer.ai "Confidence flagging" ([Drawer](https://drawer.ai/blog/11-accuracy-checks-for-automated-electrical-takeoff)).
9. **Auto-populated conditions/line items are themselves the review list.** OST "Rename and fill-out the details of Conditions" ([help](https://help.constructconnect.com/06-setting-scale-and-drawing-takeoff-including-auto-takeoff-73/on-screen-takeoff-06-02-01-takeoff-boost-auto-takeoff-overview-and-requirements-246)); PlanSwift "80% head start… You review, adjust, and price" ([blog](https://www.planswift.com/blog/your-first-ai-takeoff-in-planswift-heres-exactly-what-happens/)); Handoff "room-by-room" ([site](https://www.handoff.ai/instant-ai-estimates)).
10. **Editable polygons/points + structured feedback to the vendor.** STACK "Adjust, add or delete… Explode or Cutout" plus "Missed Something, Found Something Not There, Wrong Shape or Misidentified" ([STACK](https://www.stackct.com/floor-plan-ai/)); Buildxact "point edit tool" ([help](https://help.buildxact.com/en/articles/11363170-how-do-i-use-the-blu-takeoff-assistant)); Trimble "suggesting symbols for our AI to recognize" ([blog](https://www.trimble.com/en/blog/construction/article/stop-counting-start-winning-how-ai-is-transforming-your-estimating-workflow)).

Also emerging: natural-language counting ("Count all electrical outlets", Bluebeam Max; Trimble AI Smart Assistant) — no verification UX documented for it.

## 4. What the leaders do NOT do

- **Read room/finish/door schedules.** No documentation for STACK, Autodesk, PlanSwift/OST, Bluebeam, Trimble, Cubit, Buildxact or Beam. OST's closest step is pattern-detected GWB/ACT ceilings and "both sides of interior wall" paint area, without per-room finish typing. Only Bobyard (Jun 2026) and Kamai claim schedule joins.
- **Interior-finishing specifics.** Tile height zones, skirting minus door widths, window/door reveals, curtain niches, paint coats/coverage, per-room ceiling heights: **not found** in any vendor's documentation, including Bobyard/Kamai.
- **Wall developments, sections, elevations.** OST "does not support Detail, Section, or Elevation plans"; Buildxact "floor plans only, not roof plans or elevation"; STACK processes "one plan sheet at a time".
- **Per-item confidence and visible assumptions.** No incumbent shows either; even confidence-scoring newcomers do not expose editable assumptions (e.g., default ceiling height) — not found.
- **Validation checks on the result.** Only Bluebeam Smart Review ("missing door or plumbing fixture tags", US-commercial preview) and Buildxact's Estimate Reviewer ("common oversights", unspecified) exist.
- **Local standards.** US-centric (Bluebeam "US-based vertical commercial"; Trimble "North America, and in the UK"). DACH tools cover GAEB/VOB for BoQ text, not interior takeoff. Nothing Baltic/LT found.

## 5. What a small interior-finishing contractor (35–60, estimates by hand) would need

Grounded in the reviews above:

- **A price a one-person shop will pay.** Owners adopt at $149–$299/month (Handoff) and praise "get pricing over to customers in a much quicker time" (Stephanie H., [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/construction-software/a/handoff/)); the same segment rejects $1,250–$3,000 seats ("still asked me weekly if he had to keep using it").
- **Numbers that are never absurd.** The loudest small-contractor complaint is a confidently wrong output ("extremely high… 8 doors"), not a missed symbol. A sanity check like Buildxact's Estimate Reviewer is the pattern reviewers reward.
- **Checking that is faster than counting by hand.** Countfire's hide-counted/hide-background pass is the only documented UX that turns verification into scanning for gaps; reviewers of everything else say they "have to check very closely for any missed items". Exayard's buyer guidance: "correction speed matters more than flashy automation" ([Exayard](https://exayard.com/blog/best-ai-estimating-software)).
- **Honesty about messy PDFs.** "Quality of PDF… make it hard if the PDF is not good quality" (Adam D., Jan 2026, [SoftwareAdvice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/construction/countfire-profile/)) — say what was not read rather than fill it in.
- **His structure, not the tool's.** Room-by-room line items (Handoff, Kamai) are what reviewers describe as usable; "somewhat complicated formula system" (Kyle S., STACK) is what they resent.
- **No crashes, no relearning.** Stability and UI churn are complained about across STACK, PlanSwift and Handoff; a desktop-bound tool ("you usually have to be at your specific workstation", Kareem A., PlanSwift) is a minus.
- **Responsive human support.** Countfire's "1-on-1 training and live chat" is repeatedly praised; Cubit's paywalled support is the counter-example.

**Evidence gaps to flag:** Reddit was unreachable from this environment; no independent (non-vendor) accuracy test of any tool except Handoff's self-run benchmark was found; on-drawing overlay UX for Beam, Trimble and Kamai is undocumented publicly.
